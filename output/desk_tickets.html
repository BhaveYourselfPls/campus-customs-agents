Accounting's performance is the single best agent behaviour in the whole run. Asked whether stocking shorts would pay, it returned: "The available data cannot calculate revenue, margin, profit, payback, or whether gym shorts will sell", citing that revenue_exposure explicitly reports no sales or order table and no unit cost. A model asked for a business case will almost always produce one; producing a plausible payback period from a database with zero sales rows would have been the most dangerous single output this system could emit, because it would look exactly like a real one. The Cash tab's $0.00 line for T1 is the direct consequence of that refusal.

What costs it the full grade. Two things.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

T2 and T3 should have been one agent. Both of them, outright.

The evidence is in their own tabs. Each resolved with one delegation to one specialist, which means the five-agent structure contributed a single thing: a Boss that received a question, forwarded it verbatim to Customer Service, received a report, and restated it as a decision. Strip that out and the work is identical — call find_unmet_demand, search_products, then check_stock per candidate.

The overhead is measurable. T2 spent 12 audit steps and 13,954 tokens to produce one specialist report; T3 spent 10 steps and 11,774 tokens for one. The Boss's delegation hop costs a full extra model round-trip — read the ticket, choose a specialist, write the question, then later read the report and rewrite it as a decision — on a ticket where there was never more than one possible specialist. T2 is the sharpest case: 13,954 tokens, the highest of the three runs, for the simplest ticket, because the Boss and Customer Service each paid to load and reason over the same ticket context.

And a single agent would have fixed the T3 bug. One agent holding search, stock and catalogue tools would have seen unmatched_terms=["emerald"] and the absence of a colour synonym in one context. Instead that observation was made by Customer Service, compressed into a report, and read by a Boss who was deciding what to tell the customer — so the fix fell between the two roles. Splitting the work split the insight.

Where the five-agent structure genuinely earned its keep: T1, and only T1.

T1 is the one ticket where two specialists disagreed in kind rather than in detail. Inventory answered a merchandising question ("we carry no shorts; one request is not demand"); Accounting answered an economics question ("there is no unit cost and no sales history, so the bet cannot be sized"). Those are different refusals for different reasons, and a single agent asked "should we stock gym shorts?" would likely have produced one blended answer that quietly leaned on whichever framing came to hand. The separation also produced two independent needs_human escalations, each with its own cited reason — which is precisely the record a human needs before approving a purchase order. The Cash tab's $0.00 for T1 rests on two independent refusals, not one.

The design lesson for Campus Customs: multi-agent structure pays when a decision has genuinely separable dimensions that could conflict — buying versus economics. It is pure overhead when the ticket has exactly one owner. Two of this shop's three live tickets had exactly one owner. A sensible production build would run a single Shop Agent for fixable_by_search tickets and convene the full team only for true_assortment_gap — a routing rule find_unmet_demand already computes and hands over for free.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve today

a. Reorder triage across the 145 sold-out rows

Accounting already surfaced the raw fact on T1 without being asked: "145 sold-out product-size rows (23.7%) and 115 low-stock rows". Nobody has ever triaged them. The shop is sitting on $344,168 of stock at retail and 8,044 of sold-out exposure with no ranked list of what to reorder first.

This needs no new tools. inventory_health returns the sold-out and low-stock rows; revenue_exposure returns per-product retail value. Inventory ranks what is empty, Accounting values it, the Boss decides and flags the spend, and a human approves through POST /approvals/{id}/pay — a path already proven to work and to refuse the four wrong cases. This is the same shape as T1 but with the crucial difference that T1 lacked: these are products the shop already sells, so "will it sell?" is not the blocking question. It is the highest-value work available to this team unchanged.

b. The unmatched-terms backlog — the bug T3 left behind

find_unmet_demand already returns unmatched_terms for every failed search, and reclassifies each ask as fixable_by_search or true_assortment_gap. Run across the whole chat_messages log rather than one ticket, that is a prioritised defect list: every word a customer used that the catalogue does not know. Today it would return emerald (T3, still live), dan and handsome (T2 — the synonym works, the literal words still do not), and gym and shorts correctly separated out as a buying question, not a search one.

Customer Service and the Boss can produce that ranked list now. They cannot apply it — see problem (b) in the next section — but the triage itself is exactly what this team is good at, and it converts the T3 residual from an invisible recurring failure into a tracked work item.

c. A storefront asset sweep before Harvard–Yale weekend

Facilities was built, given check_asset_integrity, and then never consulted once across all three tickets — the ticket tabs show "not consulted" on all three boards. It is the only agent with no exercise at all.

Its job is the failure class nothing else can see: catalogue.image_file_path and chat_messages.products_json are plain strings, not foreign keys, so SQLite will never complain when they rot. A broken path is a blank tile on the storefront — the customer sees the defect, the database reports nothing wrong. With the 2025 Yale vs Harvard T-shirt in the catalogue and The Game being the shop's peak traffic event, a pre-weekend sweep of all 102 products is a genuine operational task this team can run unchanged, on a schedule, and it currently never runs.

5 · Three problems this team cannot solve

a. "Should we stock gym shorts, and how many?" — the question T1 actually asked

T1 did not answer the business question. It correctly established that the question is unanswerable with this data and escalated. Accounting's own words: "The available data cannot calculate revenue, margin, profit, payback, or whether gym shorts will sell", because revenue_exposure reports there is no sales or order table and no unit cost. The Cash tab's $0.00 is a refusal, not a decision — the shop still does not know whether to carry shorts, and running this team again will produce the same escalation forever.

What is needed: an orders / order_lines table with real sales history and a suppliers table carrying unit cost, landed cost and lead time — neither exists in campus_customs_new.db. Then MCP tools sales_history, margin_for and supplier_quote, and a new Demand Forecasting agent that can reason about seasonality — Campus Customs demand is violently seasonal around move-in, The Game and graduation, and nothing in this schema records a single date of anything being sold. Without that, Accounting's refusal is the correct permanent answer.

b. Actually fixing the emerald gap — the residual on the T3 tab

Customer Service diagnosed it exactly: unmatched_terms=["emerald"] against hoodies whose colours are gray, navy and white. Everyone agrees on the fix — add a colour synonym. Nobody can apply it. Of the seven MCP tools, six are read-only and the seventh, record_ticket_resolution, writes decisions about the shop, never the shop itself. There is no tool that can touch catalogue.colors or search_tags. The team can diagnose this defect indefinitely and close the ticket every time without ever removing it.

What is needed: a propose_catalogue_edit MCP tool that writes to a staging table rather than live, a POST /approvals/{id}/apply-edit route mirroring the payment route's human gate, and a Merchandising agent that owns catalogue vocabulary. That agent would also own the 22 dirty garment_type values the harness flagged — t-shirt vs short-sleeve T-shirt vs short-sleeve t-shirt — which quietly corrupt every category rollup Inventory produces. A colour ontology (emerald → green) plus embedding-based search would close the whole class rather than one word at a time.

c. Taking Tauhid's order — the sentence T2 wrote that nothing can honour

The T2 decision instructs Customer Service to ask Tauhid to "select an item and size before proceeding". Proceeding to what? There is no cart, no order table, no payment capture, no fulfilment.
